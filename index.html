Polder machines

Animated 3D reconstructions of Dutch water-management machines, made from technical drawings in the Provinciale Atlas Noord-Holland (Noord-Hollands Archief, collection 122). Each machine sits in its own water landscape: polder, lake, sea or canal. Around them, nature, pollution and contemporary technology go about their business.

  1. Vijzel-staartmolentje, Monnickendam 1826PolderA small tail-vane windmill driving an Archimedes screw. Built in 1826, drawn in 1836 (inv. 159).
  2. Scheprad watermolen, Schermerhorn 1816PolderAn octagonal drainage mill whose paddle wheel lifts the Beemster's water into the Schermer boezem. F. Schoorl (inv. 307).
  3. Stoomwerktuig De Leeghwater, 1843LakeOne of the steam engines that pumped the Haarlemmermeer dry: a single cylinder rocking eleven balance-beam pumps (inv. 96).
  4. Zeesluis, Muiden 1811–1812SeaA timber sea lock with mitre gates and a drawbridge between the Zuiderzee and the Vecht (inv. 359).
  5. Noorder Valdeursluis, ZaandamCanalA brick lock on the Zaan whose drop gates are lowered flat onto the floor and wound back up with chains (inv. 271).
  6. Middelste watermolen, Buitenveldert 1836Polder / cityA drainage mill whose paddle wheel was rebuilt as a three-flight screw, on land that is now the Zuidas. J. Maurik (inv. 377).
  7. Secretariegebouw, Gemeenlandshuis Delft 1931City canalThe roof of Delfland's secretariat building on the Oude Delft, drawn by H. van der Kloot Meyburg for the 1931 renovation. The slates lift to show the trusses, purlins, hips and valley (Delfland archive 116, I-A1-5-2).
Source drawings: Noord-Hollands Archief, Beeldbank, collection 122, Technische tekeningen van de Provinciale Atlas Noord-Holland.
The models are interpretive reconstructions from the drawings' proportions. Rotation speeds, gear ratios and discharges are illustrative.
